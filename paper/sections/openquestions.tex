% cannot answer today and where the next bits of attention should go.

We close with what the catalog cannot currently answer and where the
next bits of attention should go.

\paragraph{Disjoint-sum search.} The frontier-closure search of
Section \ref{sec:search} composes via Kronecker, axis concat,
recombination, peel, and pair fusion. It does \emph{not} construct
$\tau$-theorem disjoint sums; the disjoint-sum schemes the catalog
holds are imported as raw factor matrices from external
sources (Schwartz--Zwecher 2025 at large formats), never reconstructed
from a $\tau$ decomposition. We caution against reading every
external-catalog gap as a missing $\tau$ mechanism: gaps we initially
attributed to $\tau$ (including \(\nmpfield{\Rationals}{17}{17}{17}\),
where our recombination search already reaches \(2930\)) turned out to
close via ordinary recombination, not disjoint sums. A constructive
disjoint-sum operator would nonetheless let us \emph{reproduce} the
imported large-format schemes rather than carry them as opaque
matrices; its feasibility check is non-trivial -- ``does this tensor
admit a disjoint sum into these legs?'' -- and warrants a dedicated
note.

\paragraph{Salt-tolerant prediction.} Section \ref{sec:nonoverlap}
flagged that any post-construction sharing discovery (random sign
salting, column fusion, coefficient mixing) would break the
search-rank-equals-materialised-rank invariant. We believe the right
shape for adding such salt is as a separate ``polish'' phase after
the closure converges, but the interaction with the lazy
materialise design needs to be worked out before implementing.

\paragraph{\Ftwo\ promotion from \Integers.} Every integer scheme
reduces mod 2 to give an \Ftwo\ scheme; some of these reductions are
strictly tighter than the rank claimed over \Integers\ because
coefficient cancellations land. The catalog does not yet promote
integer schemes into the \Ftwo\ column automatically; doing so would
likely surface a number of ``free'' \Ftwo\ entries below the
current AlphaTensor frontier.

\paragraph{Border-rank track.} The current catalog is exact-rank
only. A border-rank track would let us include results like
Bini's \(\nmpfield{\Reals}{2}{2}{2}\) border rank \(7\)
constructions and Schönhage's $\tau$-theorem upper bounds. A
separate \code{border-bounds.json} exists and is used by the SPA
but is not yet integrated into the comparison tables.

\paragraph{Lower-bound integration.} \code{lower-bounds.json} carries
the best known lower bounds per shape -- Strassen's
\(\nmpfield{\Reals}{2}{2}{2} \ge 7\), Bl\"aser's family, the
recent Wang 2026 \(\nmpfield{\Ftwo}{3}{3}{3} \ge 20\). Adding a
``gap-to-lower-bound'' column to each comparison table would make
the SOTA frontier read more cleanly: a cell saying ``49 (R, gap 4
to lower-bound 45)'' tells more than ``49 (R)''.

\paragraph{Border between catalog and hand-crafted-discovery.} The
non-overlap property of Section \ref{sec:nonoverlap} draws a sharp
line we want to defend more carefully. Concretely: when a
hand-crafted construction lands at a shape where composition was
\emph{also} predicting the same rank, the lineage should record both
discoveries and the comparison table should report both. Today the
catalog picks one. A two-attribution column would force the
distinction into the table.

\paragraph{Reproducibility tooling.} Rather than freeze ranks into
the paper (Section \ref{ssec:no-static-ranks}), the numeric comparison
is regenerated from \code{docs/catalog.json},
\code{docs/cited-bounds.json}, and \code{docs/derived-from-cited-bounds.json} into
\code{docs/comparison/*.json} (by \code{GenerateSourceComparison}) and
rendered live by the catalog browser, per the conventions of Section
\ref{ssec:sota}. The bigger work item is finalising the SOTA
conventions document (\code{paper/sota-conventions.md}) so the
comparison has a deterministic rule for every corner case --- the
prerequisite for the exhaustive SOTA review that the curated paper
examples wait on.
