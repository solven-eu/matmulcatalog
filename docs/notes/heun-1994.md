## What the literature actually says about rank-7 ⟨2,2,2⟩

Verified references (each links to a real paper I could fetch):

1. **de Groote, H.F. (1978)**: "On varieties of optimal algorithms for
   the computation of bilinear mappings II. Optimal algorithms for
   2×2-matrix multiplication." *Theoretical Computer Science* 7.2:
   127–148.
   → **All rank-7 ⟨2,2,2⟩ algorithms over an infinite field form a
   single 9-dimensional GL₂(K)³ orbit.** This is the load-bearing
   structural theorem.

2. **Burichenko, V.P. (2014)**: "On symmetries of the Strassen algorithm",
   arXiv:1408.6273.
   → **Computes the discrete symmetry group of Strassen's algorithm:
   order 36** (or 72 for the "extended" Strassen including swap
   symmetries). Does NOT enumerate orbits of other rank-7 algorithms.

3. **Ikenmeyer, C. & Lysikov, V. (2019)**: "Strassen's 2×2 matrix
   multiplication algorithm: A conceptual perspective." *Annali
   dell'Università di Ferrara* 65(2): 241–248. arXiv:1708.08083.
   → Basis-independent proof of Strassen's algorithm. Conceptual, not a
   classification.

4. **Landsberg, J.M. & Michałek, M. (2016)**: "The geometry of rank
   decompositions of matrix multiplication I: 2×2 matrices",
   arXiv:1610.08364.
   → Geometric perspective on the rank-7 variety; not an explicit
   normal-form enumeration.

5. **Ikenmeyer, C. & Moosbauer, J. (2025)**: "Strassen's algorithm via
   orbit flip graphs", arXiv:2503.05467.
   → Uses an order-6 group action (S_3) on flip graphs to study orbits.
   **Most recent and likely most directly useful for our purposes.**

## Why no obvious "normal form list" to import

de Groote's single-orbit theorem means: in the abstract algebraic
sense, there's nothing to classify — every rank-7 ⟨2,2,2⟩ algorithm
is GL-equivalent to every other. The "discrete normal forms" question
only arises when we restrict the equivalence group to something
discrete (signed permutations, the Strassen stabilizer subgroup,
etc.), and **no paper I could find gives an explicit enumeration of
such discrete-orbit representatives**.

This contrasts with ⟨3,3,3⟩ where Smirnov, Laderman, and others list
distinct discrete schemes — but at ⟨3,3,3⟩ rank 23 there's no
GL-uniqueness theorem like de Groote's.
